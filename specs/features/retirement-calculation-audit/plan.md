## Executive verdict

**The leftover identity is actually implemented.** `project.ts:694` computes `cashFlowLeftover = cashIn − cashOut − taxResult.totalTax`, signed, every year, accumulation and retired. `employmentShareOfSavingsNet` and `employment-share.ts` are gone (the only remaining reference is a test asserting their absence), and `preRetirementAnnualNetIncome` is hydrated for debug but never read by the engine. Tyler's worked example holds in the live code: primary $10,000/year take-home, spouse out at 30, $13,024/month composed spend → leftover **−$146,288/year ≈ −$12,191/month**, `contributionsCash === 0`, taxable withdrawals > 0, spend does not shrink. After reload, the year inspector's "Monthly cash flow" row binds `cashFlowLeftover / 12` — Tyler would see roughly **−$12k** (the compact formatter rounds magnitudes ≥ $10,000; see F-9).

What is still wrong is **not** the identity — it is the *composition* feeding it. The worst confirmed bug: retirement-phase living expenses **double-subtract mortgage P&I** (once in `translateSpending`'s "mortgage paid off" adjustment, once in `toEngineMonthlySpend`'s housing carve), so any mortgaged household's retirement spend is understated by a full year of P&I and the plan looks better than it is (F-1). Second: `income_change` levers with a dollar amount (`annualDelta` or `setTo`) keep paying that income **forever**, including decades after wages end (F-2). Third: users with 3–11 months of Insights history get their 12-month *window total* treated as *annual* spending, overstating leftover up to ~4× (F-3). None of these are locked in by tests — the fixtures bypass the affected composition paths.

## Current formulas (as coded)

Real identities with file:line. These are what the code does today, not what it should do.

- **Leftover (every year):** `cashFlowLeftover = cashIn − cashOut − taxResult.totalTax` — `project.ts:694`. Signed; never clamped.
- **cashIn:** `wages + spouseWages + SS + spouseSS + rentalNet + otherIncome + insuranceIncome + oneTimeInflows` — `project.ts:614–623`. Wages gated by `age < employmentEndsAtAge` (`:288`) and `spouseAge < spouseEmploymentEndsAtAge` (`:295`), scaled by `salaryFactor`.
- **cashOut:** `livingExpenses + housingExpenses + healthcareExpenses + recurringExpenseTotal + insurancePremiums + oneTimeOutflows + otherDebtService` — `project.ts:632–639`.
- **Accumulation living:** `preRetirementAnnualSpending × (1 + expenseGrowth)^yearIndex`, where `preRetirementAnnualSpending = trailingAnnualSpending − healthcare − property P&I − property opex − insurance − other-debt` (the carve) — `build-baseline.ts:628–636`.
- **Retired living:** `toEngineMonthlySpend(estimatedRetirementMonthly, exclusions) × 12 × growth` — `build-baseline.ts:613–620` → `:174` subtracts `housingDebtServiceMonthly` **after** `translateSpending` already subtracted `mortgagePaidOffMonthly` at `spending-translation.ts:262–274` (the F-1 double-carve).
- **Housing P&I:** `(monthlyPayment + extraMonthlyPayment) × 12` while `loanBalance > 0`, not inflated — `project.ts:430–433`. Payoff via `stepProperty` (`:140`, 12 calls to `amortizeMonth`).
- **Other-debt service:** `monthlyPayment × 12` while `balance > 0 && monthlyPayment > 0` — `project.ts:625–629`. $0-payment Plaid debts stay balance-only.
- **Cash-flow tax:** accumulation skips wage/rental tax (take-home in = take-home out) and taxes only withdrawals/RMD/severance/SS; retired years tax remaining wages, SS via the two-tier provisional test (`taxes.ts:106–133`), gains at capital-gains brackets, plus flat `stateTaxRate`. Standard deduction inflated (`taxes.ts:153–168`).
- **Contribution clip:** `contributing = age < min(contributionsStopAge, employmentEndsAtAge)` (`project.ts:591–592`); `clipPlannedFromLeftover` (`:58–92`, applied `:794`) zeroes planned when leftover < 0, clips deferred → tax-free → taxable when `0 ≤ leftover < planned`.
- **Residual to cash:** `cashBalance += max(0, cashFlowLeftover + withdrawal.total − plannedApplied)` — `project.ts:812–814` — then cash grows at `inputs.cashYield`, never the portfolio return. Recorded as `contributionsCash` (`:888`).
- **Shortfall draw:** cash first (`splitTaxableWithdrawal`, `:50–57`), then taxable → deferred → Roth per `DEFAULT_WITHDRAWAL_ORDER` via `resolveWithdrawals`.
- **Monte Carlo:** identical ledger; `returnSequence[yearIndex]` overrides every investment bucket's return (`project.ts:179–180`), cash still at `cashYield`. Depletion = liquid assets < $1 **or** shortfall (stricter than deterministic funded).
- **Solvers:** earliest age (bisection), safe spending (binary search), goal seek — all call the same `projectRetirement` on cloned inputs.

### Finding detail (worked numbers)

**F-1 — mortgage double-carve (verified live).** Fixture: headline retirement spend $8,000/mo, primary-residence P&I $2,000/mo, loan alive today, payoff before longevity. `translateSpending` pushes a `mortgage-payoff` adjustment of −$2,000 → `estimatedRetirementMonthly = $6,000`. `build-baseline.ts:613` then calls `toEngineMonthlySpend($6,000, { housingDebtServiceMonthly: $2,000, … })` → engine retired living **$4,000/mo**. The engine re-adds P&I via `housingExpenses` while the loan is alive, so retired cashOut = $4,000 + $2,000 = $6,000/mo pre-payoff and $4,000/mo post-payoff — but the user said retirement lifestyle costs $8,000 including the mortgage, i.e. $6,000 excluding it. Every retired year is **$24,000/year too cheap**. Note the correct-by-construction rule "payoff improves leftover by 1× P&I" still *appears* to hold year-over-year, which is why it was invisible: the level is wrong, not the delta. Fix shape: `translateSpending` keeps the payoff adjustment for the *headline/UI narrative*, but the engine input must be `estimatedRetirementMonthly + mortgageReduction` fed to `toEngineMonthlySpend` (or equivalently drop `mortgagePaidOffMonthly` from the engine-facing figure). Add a baseline-level test with a live mortgage — the current engine tests can't see this because they hand-build `retirementMonthlySpending`.

**F-2 — income_change leak (verified live).** Lever `income_change { annualDelta: 10_000, fromAge: 40, toAge: null }`, `employmentEndsAtAge: 65`. At age 70: `earned = 0`, `adjusted = 0 × (1 + 0) + 10_000 × salaryFactor` → wages of ~$10k+ show up at 70, 80, 90. `setTo` has the same leak (`setTo × inflationFactor` regardless of employment). `percentDelta`-only levers are safe (multiply zero). Fix: skip the adjustment when the target person's wage gate is closed, unless the product wants "set income to X until Y" as a general income stream — in that case it belongs in `custom_income`, not `income_change`.

**F-3 — short-history annualization.** 6 months of data at $5,000/mo spend: `trailing.totalSpending = $30,000` → `trailingAnnualSpending = $30,000` (should be $60,000). Simultaneously `annualizeInsightsEmploymentIncome` correctly averages only months with income, so wages are right and spend is halved → leftover overstated by $30k/yr. Category rows divide by the fixed constant (`row.total / SPENDING_BASELINE_MONTHS`, `:540`), so the retirement headline is equally understated. Not visible for Tyler (12 full months) but wrong for any newer user past the 3-month gate.

**F-5 — payoff-year overcharge.** Auto loan, $500/mo, 2 payments left in the projection year: cashOut charges $6,000, amortization retires the loan after $1,000. $5,000 of phantom expense in exactly one year — small but it perturbs earliest-age/goal-seek bisection near boundaries.

## Correct, leave it

Verified against the locked product rules. Do not "fix" these.

1. **Leftover identity** — `cashIn − cashOut − cashFlowTax`, signed, on every year row (`project.ts:694`, contract doc at `packages/contracts/src/retirement.ts:662–663`). The overview (empty-lever) path and the levered path produce the identical identity.
2. **Tyler's worked example** — $10k treated as **annual**; −$12,191/month; no contributions; spend does not shrink when the spouse stops working; asserted in `retirement-engine.test.ts` ("uses wages minus composed spend when the spouse stops working at 30") including the explicit anti-assertion against the −$3,024 "monthly" fail case.
3. **Superseded identity is dead** — `employment-share.ts` deleted; `grepping packages/` finds `employmentShareOfSavingsNet` only inside the test that asserts it is not imported; `preRetirementAnnualNetIncome` unread by the engine (doc comment in `types.ts` says so, and no UI reads it).
4. **Residual → cash, not stocks** — planned 401(k)/Roth/brokerage compound at their buckets; residual lands in `cashBalance` before cash yield (`project.ts:812–814`); post-retirement surplus also goes to cash. Locked by "adds residual leftover to cash before applying cash yield", "lands post-retirement surplus in cash not taxable", "compounds only planned taxable contributions at the stock return".
5. **Clip rules** — leftover < 0 ⇒ no contributions; `0 ≤ leftover < planned` ⇒ clip deferred → tax-free → taxable; leftover > planned ⇒ planned in full, residual to cash. All three locked by tests.
6. **Withdrawal order** — cash first (`splitTaxableWithdrawal`), then taxable → deferred → Roth; leftover never clamped. Locked by "withdraws cash before taxable stocks when leftover is negative".
7. **Mortgage carve (accumulation)** — P&I, opex, healthcare, insurance, other-debt carved out of trailing living exactly once (`build-baseline.ts:628–636`); P&I does not inflate; living inflates with expense growth; payoff improves leftover by 1× P&I (locked by "improves leftover by P&I not 2x P&I"). The double-carve (F-1) is **retired-phase only**.
8. **Other-debt** — carved from living, re-added as `otherDebtService`, drops at payoff (locked by test); mortgages excluded from `liabilities` when a property models them, with balance-match dedupe between profile and Plaid rows (`assemble-liabilities.ts`); Plaid $0-payment debts stay balance-only by design (locked by "keeps unmatched otherDebt flat").
9. **Accumulation tax skip** — wages/rental untaxed (take-home basis); withdrawals/RMD/SS/severance taxed; retired years tax remaining wages including a still-working spouse. RMD parked in cash only when leftover ≥ 0, tax already netted — no double-add on draw years (traced through `settleTaxes`, `additional` resolved against balance minus RMD).
10. **Cash yield** — single household `cashYield`; `weightedCashYield` uses only user-edited account rates and never lets the Plaid 0.07 schema default masquerade as a cash rate; cash grows at `cashYield` in deterministic *and* Monte Carlo paths (locked by tests).
11. **Scenario-local levers** — `applyLevers` clones inputs, never mutates baseline; all **62** contract lever types have matching engine branches (62/62, no drift); scenarios persist `{ name, levers }` only (Prisma schema has no balance snapshot); reopen = today's baseline + saved stack. No lever writes Financial Profile, Insights, or Plaid — confirmed across domain, API handlers, and desktop IPC.
12. **Edit-plan cascade** — `handleSave` → `updateRetirementPlan` → `writeOverlappingRatesToBothStores` → RE/investments → query invalidation; skip-if-unchanged protects the ForecastScenario; cash yield stays profile-only and is not run through `ratePercentToDecimal`. What-If never touches this path.
13. **UI unit bindings** — "Monthly cash flow" = `cashFlowLeftover / 12` signed (`year-inspector.tsx:76–79`); "Added to savings" = annual `contributionsCash`; chart series `other` labeled **Cash** with the retirement dotted line; income split labeled annual take-home with copy stating spend independence; suggested/spouse cards free of "Removes $X/year" and wage-share language; impact/compare read projection deltas only.
14. **Hosts are thin** — API handlers Zod-parse → service → domain, correct `withAuth`/`withDemoOrAuth` wrappers on all 16 endpoints; desktop IPC mirrors them; ForecastScenario rates are read-only inputs to the baseline; `getNetIncome` is the single shared Insights math (12-month trailing window, `net = income − spending`).
15. **Solvers** — earliest-age bisection, safe spending, goal seek, stress, improve-plan all wrap the same `projectRetirement`; opportunity ranking uses measured projection deltas, not hardcoded scores.
16. **Monte Carlo strict depletion** (liquid < $1 counts as failure even without shortfall) is an intentional, test-locked design choice, not a bug — but see Open Questions if Tyler wants the definitions unified.

## Architecture

**Functional Core, Imperative Shell is the right call, and the codebase is already ~80% there.** `projectRetirement` is pure (no DB, no Prisma, no global state), `applyLevers` clones, and the shell (`build-baseline.ts` + hosts) owns all I/O. Don't restructure the pipeline; finish the decomposition:

1. **Extract per-line calculators from the year loop.** The loop body in `project.ts` is several hundred lines of interleaved concerns. Break it into the pure functions Tyler sketched — `calculateWages`, `calculateHousing`, `calculateOtherDebtService`, `calculateCashIn`, `calculateCashOut`, `calculateCashFlowTax`, `calculateLeftover`, `calculateContributions` (subsumes `clipPlannedFromLeftover`), `calculateWithdrawals`, `applyYearEndGrowth` — each `(YearContext) => output`, unit-testable in isolation. `calculateCashFlowLeftover` becomes the literal three-line identity. This directly prevents the F-1/F-2 class: F-2 exists because the income-adjustment loop is inlined *after* the wage gate instead of inside a `calculateWages` that owns gating.
2. **One spend-composition module.** F-1 and F-4 are the same disease: carve-outs applied at two layers (`spending-translation.ts` and `toEngineMonthlySpend`) with no shared ownership. Create a single pure `composeSpend(headline, carveOuts) → { living, housing, healthcare, insurance, otherDebt }` partition whose parts provably sum to the headline, used by both the accumulation and retired paths and by the inspector's reallocation copy. Property-test it: `sum(partition) === headline` for arbitrary inputs.
3. **Lever registry instead of a 62-arm switch.** Each lever becomes a colocated module `{ schema, apply(inputs, params), describe, defaultParams, conflictField }` registered in a map; `apply-levers.ts` folds the registry. Adding scenario #63 touches one new file, not four (`contracts`, `apply-levers`, `describe-lever`, `default-lever`). The contract union can be generated from the registry to keep the 62/62 parity mechanical.
4. **Golden-fixture regression tests.** Two frozen fixtures — Tyler's live example and the second interaction fixture (inflation + 401(k) + RMD + two mortgages + other-debt) — snapshot every year-row field. Any identity change shows up as a reviewable diff instead of a silent drift. Plus an invariant test run over every fixture: for each year, `cashFlowLeftover === cashIn − cashOut − taxesPaid(cash-flow portion)` and `essential + flexible + housing + healthcare + … === totalOutflow`.
5. **Keep the shell honest.** `build-baseline.ts` is the largest remaining imperative surface; split its pure math (annualization, carve-outs, employment-pie resolution) from its I/O (repo reads) so the math half is testable with plain objects — that is where F-3 lives and why no test caught it.

## Open questions for Tyler

1. **Contributions when only the spouse works (F-7).** Today `contributing` dies with the **primary's** `employmentEndsAtAge`. If you retire at 50 and your spouse works to 65, should household planned contributions (or the spouse's share) continue? Current behavior: they stop.
2. **Accumulation tax on withdrawals vs the −$12,191 identity (F-8).** With a state rate > 0 (or once cash is exhausted and brokerage draws realize gains), withdrawal tax makes leftover *more* negative than the pure `10k/12 − 13,024` figure. That is arguably correct behavior — but confirm the worked example is "≈ −$12,191 before withdrawal tax," not an exact invariant, so future tests don't freeze the wrong number. Related: should wages consume the standard deduction before withdrawal income during accumulation (more tax), or is today's lenient treatment fine for v1?
3. **`income_change` semantics (F-2 fix shape).** Should a `setTo`/`annualDelta` with no `toAge` end at employment end (recommended), or is "income of $X until age Y regardless of employment" a real use case that belongs in `custom_income`?
4. **Monte Carlo "depleted" vs deterministic "funded."** MC fails a trial when liquid assets < $1 even with no shortfall; the deterministic ledger only fails on shortfall. Intentional conservatism (test-locked) — keep, or unify the definitions so the headline and confidence can't disagree at the margin?
5. **Underwater property (F-10).** Keep the `max(0, equity)` clamp (net worth never shows negative equity) or show signed equity?
6. **Severance** is taxed as ordinary income during accumulation — is the lever amount intended as gross (tax it, current behavior) or take-home (skip it, like wages)?
